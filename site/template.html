    Data sources

    Where every number on this page comes from. The archive is downloadable and described in datapackage.json.

    LA Metro bus timetables (GTFS)
    Every published version of Metro's bus schedule since 2016. Rebuilt into scheduled speeds by hour.
    LA Metro realtime API
    Live bus positions every few seconds for lines 204, 754, 2 and 4. Open, no key.
    Swiftly realtime API
    Metro's realtime data provider: every bus once a minute, plus predicted arrivals. Keyed access.
    TomTom Traffic API (Orbis v2)
    General-traffic speed on each piece of road, collected every 15 minutes and matched to each street. Keyed access.
    Cal-ITP average transit speeds
    Caltrans' measured bus speeds by stop for one recent weekday. A cross-check on the timetable.
    GBFS shared bikes and scooters
    Public availability feeds from Bird and Metro Bike Share, recorded every 10 minutes. Not yet charted.
    Open Mobility Foundation MDS
    The standard used to export the bus GPS positions as vehicle telemetry.
    Crowdsourced (planned)
    Counts of cars, bikes and people collected by residents and community groups, without identifying anyone. See the options below.

    Crowdsourced data

    Counts collected by residents and community groups fill gaps that bus and traffic feeds leave, such as Fountain Av, where no bus runs, and the number of people walking and biking a road diet is meant to bring. Every option here counts without identifying people or vehicles.

    Telraam
    A small counter inside a window facing the street counts cars, heavy vehicles, bikes and pedestrians and measures car speeds. Counting happens on the device and no images are kept. Counts are published openly by street segment. First choice for Fountain Av and Sunset Bl by the Vista Theatre, before construction starts.
    OpenTrafficCam
    Open-source video counting: a do-it-yourself camera records the street, then software counts road users, turning movements and speeds. Suited to short before-and-after studies at one intersection, such as Hollywood, Sunset and Hillhurst. Video is kept until analysed, so it needs a clear deletion policy.
    Strava Metro
    Bike and run trips by street segment from app users, free for agencies and approved researchers. Skews toward sport riders.
    Volunteer counts and speed checks
    Manual bike and pedestrian counts on set days, and spot speed checks on a block, by local groups and residents. Simple, but needs a consistent method to compare before and after.

    In practice: on Lake Street in San Francisco, residents used window counters to make the case for keeping a slow street (Streetsblog), and Transpo Maps shows Bay Area counters on a live public map.

## Three delay measures

- **Road travel time above free flow.** The pinned November rollups contain **19,902 numeric hourly pairs** in 20,995 rows (TomTom 13,727/14,169 and HERE 6,175/6,826). A numeric pair has positive, finite current speed, baseline speed, travel time and length. Even one timestamp, full reported coverage and zero closure can conceal different link support. The count of truly paired observations is unknown. For a pilot, inventory expected, attempted, successful and usable polls separately. For each canonical poll and directional section, compute both travel times as `60 × Σ(miles / mph)` over the same deduplicated open intervals, record common distance and missing or closed distance, and retain signed differences. Aggregate a corridor only across poll IDs shared by all required sections. Keep closure status and changing footprints explicit. The pilot decision awaits December provider-rights review. [Pinned traffic builder](https://github.com/here/aviary/blob/6636c6c/pipeline/build_traffic.py)
- **Bus timetable deviation.** Match verified actual arrival or departure to the same scheduled event by applicable GTFS revision, trip instance, service date and stop occurrence. Actual minus scheduled is positive for late events and negative for early events. The audit found 577 overlapping hourly keys and zero verifiable matched stop events. The eligible stop-event denominator is unknown. [Offline validation and actual-event requirements](../validation/TRIP_STOP_MATCHING.md)
- **Incident disruption estimate.** The pinned TomTom rollup has **760 numeric maxima in 764 incident-section-direction rows** (536 of 540 distinct provider IDs). All numeric rows are jams. The full incident estimate can appear on multiple section rows, so summing rows overcounts disruption. Three closure rows and one roadworks row have missing delay. The reducer treats explicit zero like missing and does not preserve the poll containing the peak. Recheck zero semantics and provenance before adding an incident delay display. Injury severity comes from crash reports.
